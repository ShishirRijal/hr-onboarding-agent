## 1. Company Mission and Values

**Section: Company Mission**

NordTech Solutions builds workforce management software that helps mid-sized companies run HR operations without the complexity of enterprise-grade tools. Our mission is to make people-operations work — leave, scheduling, compliance, and performance tracking — feel effortless for both HR teams and the employees they support.

**Section: Company Values**

- **Direct communication.** In the Nordic and Baltic tradition, we say what we mean. Feedback is given plainly and early, not softened into vagueness or delayed until a formal review.
- **Flat hierarchy.** Titles exist for clarity of responsibility, not for gatekeeping decisions. Any employee can raise an idea or concern directly with a manager, the executive team, or a peer in another function.
- **Work-life balance.** We do not equate hours worked with value delivered. Employees are expected to manage their time responsibly and disconnect outside working hours.
- **Async-first collaboration.** With three offices across different time zones (Tallinn, Porto, Tampere), we default to written, asynchronous communication over meetings wherever possible.
- **Ownership.** Employees are trusted to make decisions within their domain without needing sign-off for every step.

## 2. Code of Conduct

**Section: Code of Conduct**

All NordTech employees are expected to:

1. Treat colleagues, customers, and partners with respect regardless of role, seniority, nationality, or background.
2. Avoid discrimination or harassment of any kind, including based on gender, age, ethnicity, religion, disability, or sexual orientation.
3. Maintain confidentiality of customer data and internal company information (see Section 8, Data Protection Responsibilities, for specifics relevant to GDPR).
4. Disclose any conflicts of interest to their manager or HR — for example, a personal relationship with a vendor or competitor.
5. Report violations of this Code of Conduct to their manager, HR, or, if the concern involves their own manager, directly to Eva Kallas (Head of HR) or any member of the executive team.

Violations of the Code of Conduct are handled through the disciplinary process described in Section 5.

## 3. Communication Norms

**Section: Communication Norms — Slack Etiquette**

- Use threads for replies to keep channels readable — avoid posting a new top-level message when replying to an existing discussion.
- Default to public channels over DMs where the conversation could benefit others; use DMs for genuinely private or sensitive matters.
- Respect time zones. NordTech spans Estonia, Portugal, and Finland (a maximum spread of about 2 hours), but employees also work remotely from other locations. Do not expect immediate replies outside someone's working hours, and mark messages as non-urgent unless truly time-sensitive.
- Use "@here" and "@channel" sparingly — reserve them for messages that genuinely need everyone's attention right now.

**Section: Communication Norms — Meeting Culture**

- Every meeting should have an agenda shared in advance. Meetings without a clear agenda may be declined by invitees.
- Default meeting length is 25 or 50 minutes (not 30 or 60) to leave a buffer between back-to-back meetings.
- Meetings should have a clear owner who documents decisions and action items in Notion afterward, so anyone unable to attend can catch up asynchronously.
- Recurring meetings are reviewed quarterly — if a meeting hasn't produced value, cancel it rather than letting it run on habit.

**Section: Communication Norms — Async-First Principles**

- Default to writing things down in Notion or Linear rather than relying on verbal or Slack-only decisions that can get lost.
- A meeting should be the last resort for a decision, not the first — try async written discussion first, and escalate to a call only if async discussion stalls.
- Because NordTech spans three offices and remote employees across multiple time zones, async-first communication is not optional — it's the default operating mode.

## 4. Performance Review Process

**Section: Performance Review Process**

NordTech runs formal performance reviews **twice a year**, in **May and November**.

1. Each review cycle begins with a self-assessment, submitted by the employee, followed by peer feedback gathered from 2–4 colleagues nominated by the employee and their manager together.
2. The manager synthesizes self-assessment and peer feedback into a written review, which is discussed in a dedicated 1:1 meeting.
3. Reviews assess performance against role expectations set at the start of the cycle, not against colleagues — NordTech does not use forced ranking or a bell curve.
4. Outcomes from the review cycle may include a compensation adjustment, promotion discussion, or a development plan, depending on the individual's situation. (Note: this handbook does not cover the detailed salary review process or raise timing — for specifics, contact your manager or HR directly.)
5. New employees have their first formal review at the next scheduled cycle after their 90-day probation review (see the Onboarding Checklist), not before.

## 5. Disciplinary Process

**Section: Disciplinary Process**

NordTech follows a structured, fair disciplinary process for conduct or performance issues that fall outside normal coaching conversations:

1. **Informal conversation.** Most issues are first addressed informally between the employee and manager, focused on understanding the issue and agreeing on next steps.
2. **Verbal warning.** If the issue persists or is more serious, a documented verbal warning is given, with clear expectations for improvement and a defined timeframe (typically 2–4 weeks).
3. **Written warning.** Continued or more serious issues result in a formal written warning, involving HR, with a documented improvement plan.
4. **Final written warning.** A further escalation if the issue is not resolved, again involving HR, with explicit consequences outlined if the issue continues.
5. **Termination.** As a last resort, if the issue remains unresolved after the above steps, employment may be terminated per Section 6.

Serious violations (e.g., harassment, fraud, gross misconduct) may bypass earlier steps and proceed directly to a final warning or termination, at HR and management's discretion, following applicable local labour law.

## 6. Termination Policy

**Section: Termination Policy**

- Termination can occur voluntarily (resignation) or involuntarily (dismissal, redundancy, or end of a disciplinary process).
- Notice periods are governed by local labour law in the employee's country of employment (Estonia, Finland, or Portugal) and by the terms of the individual's employment contract — this handbook does not override statutory minimums.
- Employees who resign should provide notice in writing to their manager and HR, following the notice period specified in their contract.
- Upon termination, IT will deactivate company accounts and collect company equipment (see the IT Setup Guide for the systems affected); HR will process final payroll and any applicable severance in line with local law.
- This handbook does not cover redundancy-specific questions such as treatment of unvested stock options — those are addressed on a case-by-case basis by HR and Finance.

## 7. Remote Work Policy

**Section: Remote Work Policy**

- Employees may work **up to 3 days per week from home** by default, with the remaining days expected in their assigned office.
- Employees may work **fully remotely** if agreed individually with their manager — this is assessed based on role requirements, team needs, and individual circumstances, rather than being a blanket entitlement.
- Remote work does not change an employee's registered country of employment for payroll, tax, or benefits purposes; working from a different country for an extended period requires prior approval (see Section 8 for the data protection implications, and note that this handbook does not fully resolve extended cross-border remote work — check with HR before making plans).
- Employees working remotely are still expected to be reachable during their core working hours and to attend key meetings (team standups, the Monday company sync) as though they were in the office.
- Full remote-work eligibility does not affect the home office setup benefit described in the Benefits & Perks Guide, which is available to eligible remote employees after 6 months of employment.

## 8. Data Protection Responsibilities

**Section: Data Protection Responsibilities**

Because NordTech's product handles HR data for our customers' employees, every NordTech employee has data protection responsibilities under GDPR, regardless of role:

1. Only access customer data that is necessary for your specific task — do not browse or query customer data out of curiosity.
2. Never export customer data to personal devices, personal cloud storage, or unapproved third-party tools.
3. Report any suspected data breach — including a lost device, phishing compromise, or accidental data exposure — to IT **within 2 hours of discovery** (see the GDPR Employee Data Policy for the full breach procedure).
4. Follow the internal AI use policy when using tools like ChatGPT or Claude for work tasks: never paste customer personally identifiable information into external AI tools (see the EU AI Act Compliance Summary for full detail).
5. Complete the mandatory data protection component of security training during Week 1 of onboarding (see the Onboarding Checklist).

Full detail on what personal data NordTech collects, retention periods, and employee rights is covered in the dedicated GDPR Employee Data Policy — this handbook provides only a summary of day-to-day responsibilities.

**Section: Travel Policy Overview**

- Business travel between NordTech offices (Tallinn, Porto, Tampere) or to customer sites should be booked with cost-consciousness in mind, following the caps and approval rules in the Expense & Reimbursement Policy.
- All travel expenses — flights, hotels, meals — are governed by the Expense & Reimbursement Policy; this handbook does not restate those figures. Refer there for exact allowances (e.g., meal and hotel caps) and the flight class policy.
