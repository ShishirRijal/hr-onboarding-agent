## 1. Background

**Section: Background**

The EU Artificial Intelligence Act entered into force on 12 July 2024 and takes a **risk-based approach**: obligations scale with how much potential harm an AI system poses, rather than applying one uniform rule to all AI. Prohibitions on the highest-risk practices have applied since **2 February 2025**, with obligations for high-risk systems phasing in on a longer timeline through 2026–2027. Because NordTech builds workforce management software — a category the Act treats with particular scrutiny when it touches employment decisions — this summary explains how our own products, and our employees' day-to-day use of AI tools, fit into the Act's framework.

## 2. The Four Risk Categories

**Section: Risk Categories Overview**

The Act sorts AI systems into four tiers:

1. **Unacceptable risk — banned outright.** This includes AI systems using subliminal manipulation, exploiting vulnerabilities of specific groups, social scoring, and other practices judged incompatible with EU fundamental rights. NordTech does not build or use any system in this category.
2. **High-risk — permitted but heavily regulated.** This covers AI systems used in specified sensitive domains — including **employment, worker management, and access to self-employment** — such as systems used for recruitment, candidate screening, or decisions affecting promotion, termination, or task allocation. High-risk systems require conformity assessments, technical documentation, human oversight mechanisms, and registration before deployment.
3. **Limited risk — transparency obligations.** Systems such as chatbots must clearly disclose to users that they are interacting with AI, but face no further mandatory obligations beyond that disclosure.
4. **Minimal risk — no mandatory obligations.** The large majority of everyday AI-enabled features fall here, with only voluntary codes of conduct encouraged.

## 3. Classification of NordTech's Products

**Section: NordTech Product Classification**

**Core HR/workforce management software — Limited Risk.** NordTech's core product (leave management, scheduling, org-structure tracking, and general workforce administration) does not make or materially influence decisions about hiring, promotion, or termination on its own — it primarily automates administrative workflows and surfaces information to human HR staff and managers, who retain full decision-making authority. As such, this core product is classified as **limited risk**, subject to transparency obligations (e.g., clearly labeling any AI-generated content or suggestions within the product) but not the full high-risk compliance regime.

**Recruitment screening features — High Risk.** Any feature that screens, ranks, filters, or otherwise evaluates job candidates — including any AI-assisted resume screening or candidate-matching functionality NordTech offers or considers offering — falls squarely within the Act's Annex III high-risk category for employment and worker management. Any such feature requires a full conformity assessment, documented human oversight, bias testing, and registration before it can be offered to customers. As of this document's publication, NordTech does not offer AI-based recruitment screening as a live product feature; any future development in this area must go through Legal and Compliance review before release.

## 4. Employee Obligations When Using AI Tools

**Section: Employee Obligations**

All employees who build, configure, or use AI features — whether in NordTech's own product or in day-to-day work tools — must:

1. Understand which risk category applies to any AI feature they are working on, and flag to Legal/Compliance if a feature might touch high-risk employment use cases (recruitment, promotion, termination, performance-based task allocation).
2. Ensure any AI-generated output shown to end users (customers or their employees) is clearly labeled as AI-assisted where required.
3. Never deploy an AI feature that makes a final, unreviewed decision about a person's employment (hiring, firing, promotion, discipline) without a human in the loop.
4. Escalate any AI system idea that would process special category data (health data, biometric data, etc.) to Legal/Compliance before development begins, so appropriate safeguards can be designed in from the start.

## 5. What Employees Must NOT Do

**Section: Prohibited Practices**

- **Do not** allow an AI system to make a final hiring, promotion, or termination decision on its own, without meaningful human review. AI output can inform a decision, but a person must make it.
- **Do not** use AI systems to process special category data (health data, biometric data, data revealing ethnicity, religion, or similar) without documented safeguards approved by Legal/Compliance.
- **Do not** build or ship any candidate-screening or workforce-monitoring feature without first classifying it against Section 3 and completing the required review — even a well-intentioned feature can trigger high-risk obligations.
- **Do not** assume a feature is "just a suggestion tool" and therefore exempt — the Act looks at the practical effect on the individual, not the marketing description of the feature.

## 6. Internal AI Use Policy (Employee Tools)

**Section: Internal AI Use Policy**

Separate from what NordTech builds into its product, this section covers employees' everyday use of AI assistants (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude) for internal work tasks:

1. Using ChatGPT, Claude, or similar AI assistants for internal work tasks — drafting, summarizing, coding assistance, research — is **permitted** and encouraged where it improves productivity.
2. **No customer personally identifiable information (PII)** may be entered into external AI tools. This includes customer employee names, contact details, health data, or any other identifying information belonging to NordTech's customers or their employees.
3. Internal NordTech data (non-customer, non-sensitive) can generally be used with approved external AI tools, but employees should still avoid pasting confidential business information (e.g., unreleased financials, unannounced product plans) into tools outside NordTech's control.
4. Questions about whether a specific use case is permitted should be directed to your manager or Legal/Compliance before proceeding, rather than assumed.
